## Open sources for the practical block (YCB1 workshop deck)

Scope: the two files ycb1_practical_demoskills.tex and ycb1_practical_asanas.tex. Findings are in my own words. Reliability tags: Board-named means the book is on the Board's own Level 1 practicum reference list (Brahmachari, MDNIY monographs, Satyananda Saraswati / Bihar School of Yoga). Secondary means a reprint, a school page or a general site.

## Item 1. Sarvanga Pushti and Hrid Gati

Found in deck: frames "Sarvanga Pushti" and "Hrid Gati (Engine Daud)" had only generic lines (full-body strength, cardio like running) and a cautions line with no source.

Found on web:
- Source: text attributed to Swami Dhirendra Brahmachari, Yogic Suksma Vyayama and Yogic Sthula Vyayama, edited reprint (Mahesh Chand, for the Japan Fitness Yoga Association). https://dev.in.yoga/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/Yogic-sukshma-vyayam.pdf (scanned PDF, text extracted locally). Board-named author, but a reprint: confirm wording against the original or the MDNIY monograph before the exam copy is final.
- Sarvanga pushti (whole body development): feet as wide apart as possible, fists with thumbs tucked in, one wrist on the other, bend towards one ankle. Raise the joined arms backward and forward in a circle with the trunk and bring them down towards the other ankle. Done very slowly. The text gives breathing through the nose (inhale coming down and exhale rising, which reads oddly), so teach "with the breath" and let a certified teacher fix the pattern. Number of rounds not given.
- Hrid gati (yogic jogging, called the locomotive exercise): feet together, elbows bent, fists with thumbs tucked in. Run about fifty small steps forward on the toes, heels flicking back towards the buttocks, arms thrust forward and back alternately like pistons, then the same distance backward. Sharp deep nose breathing that gives an engine-like hiss.
- Benefit statements in that text (cures stiff backs, tuberculosis, obesity) are cure claims and were not used.
- No caution list for either practice was found in this text. The deck keeps a general precaution (take expert advice with high blood pressure, heart conditions, back or neck pain).

Applied to deck: steps and breath lines added to both frames, benefits softened to "traditionally credited".

Still open: rounds or counts for sarvanga pushti; a Board or MDNIY statement of cautions; original Brahmachari text or MDNIY monograph (Basavaraddi, 2016) to confirm the wording.

## Item 2. Hasta mudras: chin, chinmaya, brahma, adi, dhyana; jnana versus chin palm direction

Found in deck: frame "Hasta Mudras (Hand Gestures)" gave all seven with forms taken from a school site (Himalayan Yoga Academy), with jnana palms down and chin palms up, and health claims for brahma and adi.

Found on web (Niranjananand Saraswati, Pran and Pranayam, Bihar School of Yoga / Yoga Publications Trust, Bihar School line, on a text mirror):
- Chinmaya mudra: hold the fingers as for jnana mudra, then fold the three straight fingers in so their tips touch or point to the palm; the index tip may touch the base or the tip of the thumb; hands on the knees, palms up or down. https://jainqq.org/pagetext/Pran_and_Pranayam/009371/319
- Adi mudra: fold the thumb into the palm and curl the fingers over it to make a fist; hands on the knees, palms up or down. Same page.
- Brahma mudra: thumbs turned inward across the palms, fingers folded over them; backs of the hands on the thighs, knuckles touching, held close to the body at pelvic level. https://jainqq.org/pagetext/Pran_and_Pranayam/009371/320
- Jnana and chin: this text says the index tip touches the thumb in both, ties chin to abdominal breathing, and does not state palm direction on the pages read. https://jainqq.org/pagetext/Pran_and_Pranayam/009371/318
- Common Yoga Protocol (Board and AYUSH): jnana mudra is thumb tip and index tip in a circle, other three fingers straight and together, palms up on the thighs. Nasagra mudra: index and middle folded, ring and little finger by the left nostril, thumb by the right.
- Search results (non-Bihar, secondary) say chin mudra is jnana with palms up; others use the names interchangeably. No source reached states a Board difference.

Applied to deck: jnana changed to the protocol form (palms up, some texts palms down); chin changed to "same contact as jnana, sources differ"; chinmaya, adi, brahma re-worded from the Bihar School text with the source named; health claims removed; nasika matched to the protocol's nasagra mudra.

Still open: dhyana mudra (the protocol meditates in jnana mudra, the slide's palms-stacked form has no source); a statement of the chin versus jnana difference by the Board or MDNIY; whether the Board wants brahma mudra at the navel or the pelvic level.

## Item 3. Pranava japa, soham, body and breath awareness, yoga nidra

Found in deck: frames "Concept of Pranava", "Concept of Hymns" and "Practices Leading to Meditation and Dhyana Sadhana". None gives a technique for soham, body and breath awareness or yoga nidra.

Found on web:
- Yoga nidra (Satyananda method) in outline: internalisation, resolve (sankalpa), rotation of consciousness through body parts without concentrating, breath awareness without altering the breath, opposites, visualisation, repeated resolve, externalisation. Secondary only: https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoganidra . The primary book is Satyananda Saraswati, Yoga Nidra (Bihar School of Yoga, 1976); not read. Note: Wikipedia states this staged form is not found in ancient or medieval texts.
- Common Yoga Protocol (the Board's practice reference) teaches meditation in jnana mudra with mild attention between the eyebrows and awareness of the breath, and ends with sankalpa and shanti path. It does not teach pranava, soham or yoga nidra.
- Pranava: the Guidebook (following Patanjali) names pranava as the word for Ishvara, with japa done with its meaning in mind. No step-by-step chanting technique found.
- Soham and ajapa japa: nothing usable found in a Board or MDNIY text. NIOS lists a meditation practical (digital.nios.ac.in/topic.php?id=373en) but its text was not retrieved.

Applied to deck: pranava and hymn frames reworded as tradition with the Guidebook and protocol behind them; the meditation preparation list marked as a teaching sequence, not the Board order. No soham, awareness or nidra technique added.

Still open: soham and ajapa japa steps; a body-and-breath-awareness script; a Board-approved yoga nidra script (MDNIY Yoga Teachers Manual for School Teachers, or Satyananda Yoga Nidra); chanting method for pranava (number of rounds, pitch).

## Item 4. Uddiyana and maha bandha rules and cautions

Found in deck: frame "Bandhas (Internal Locks)" named the three bandhas with "regulates energy" lines. Maha bandha is not in the Board list (the three bandhas are) but appears in a comment.

Found on web:
- Uddiyana bandha, Satyananda Saraswati, Asana Pranayama Mudra Bandha (Bihar School; Board-named). https://jainqq.org/pagetext/Asan_Pranayam_Mudra_Bandh/009366/421 : lean forward with palms on the knees, straighten the elbows, apply jalandhara, draw the abdomen in and up and hold with the breath held out, release, then inhale slowly; rest between rounds. Start with three rounds and build slowly to ten over months. Easier after an inverted asana. Advanced: learn under guidance after skill in breath retention, jalandhara and mula bandha. Avoid with colitis, stomach or intestinal ulcer, diaphragmatic hernia, high blood pressure, heart disease, glaucoma, raised intracranial pressure, and in pregnancy.
- Uddiyana bandha, NIOS, seated version: sit upright in a meditative posture, inhale, exhale fully, apply jalandhara, lift the upper abdomen under the rib cage, hold as long as comfortable. Avoid with high blood pressure, heart disease, hernia, glaucoma, pregnancy, menstruation, duodenal or peptic ulcer. https://digital.nios.ac.in/content/373en/Book-2-373/files/basic-html/page137.html
- Maha bandha, same Bihar School book: sit in siddha, siddha yoni or padmasana, hands on knees. Inhale, exhale forcefully and completely, hold the breath out, apply jalandhara, uddiyana and mula bandha in that order, then release in reverse order, then inhale. Shift awareness through the perineum, abdomen and throat. One round at first, adding one round at a time up to nine. Learn the three bandhas first. Avoid with high or low blood pressure, heart conditions, stroke history, hernia, ulcer, recovery from a visceral illness, pregnancy. https://jainqq.org/pagetext/Asan_Pranayam_Mudra_Bandh/009366/423 and https://jainqq.org/pagetext/Asan_Pranayam_Mudra_Bandh/009366/424
- Hatha Pradipika (third chapter) as already confirmed in the verdict for jalandhara and mula bandha.

Applied to deck: one caution line (advanced, qualified teacher, empty stomach, the conditions above) and one-line descriptions of the three bandhas. No step list added to the deck.

Still open: Board or MDNIY wording; the Hatha Pradipika verses for maha bandha; whether the Board examines maha bandha at all (the syllabus lists only the three).

## Item 5. Antar and bahir trataka

Found in deck: frame "Trataka" (internal trataka at the trikuti, external at "objects that provide pleasure").

Found on web:
- Satyananda Saraswati, Asana Pranayama Mudra Bandha, Bihar School: gaze at a fixed object without blinking, effortlessly; a candle flame is usual, a black dot for eye ailments (eyestrain, myopia, astigmatism, early cataract); epileptics should not gaze at a flame; avoid sun, moon, mirror, crystal ball. The pages read do not give the antar and bahir split. https://jainqq.org/pagetext/Asan_Pranayam_Mudra_Bandh/009366/516
- NIOS practical manual: flame at eye level about four feet away, sit in padmasana or sukhasana, gaze without blinking until the eyes tire, burn or water, close the eyes, repeat; do not go beyond twenty minutes; epileptic persons need expert advice. https://digital.nios.ac.in/content/373en/Pract/30.pdf
- Internal (antar) trataka as closing the eyes and holding the afterimage between the eyebrows, and external (bahir) as gazing at the flame: stated by commercial and yoga-school sites only (secondary), not by a Board text.

Applied to deck: external trataka reworded to a candle flame or small mark; epilepsy caution added; the antar and bahir split labelled a common teaching split, not in the Guidebook.

Still open: Gheranda Samhita or Hatha Pradipika trataka verses; a Board or MDNIY statement of the antar and bahir split and duration.

## Item 6. Remaining sukshma vyayama steps and the unsourced asanas

Found in deck: frames for shoulder, trunk, knee, thigh and ankle movements, and asana frames for dandasana, sukhasana, mandukasana, uttana mandukasana, paschimottanasana, purvottanasana, gomukhasana, vipareetakarani, saral matsyasana, kati chakrasana.

Found on web (Brahmachari reprint, https://dev.in.yoga/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/Yogic-sukshma-vyayam.pdf):
- Bhuja valli: feet together, raise one arm sideways overhead, palm open and outward, fingers together, bring it down without touching head or thigh; right, left, then both together.
- Purna bhuja: fists, thumbs tucked in; swing one arm in a full circle forward, then in reverse; then the other arm; then both arms in full circles.
- Kati shakti vikasaka, five forms: first, fist behind the back with the other hand on the wrist, bend back then forward, head towards the knees; second, legs wide apart, hands on hips, bend back then forward, head to the ground; third, feet together, quick back and forward bends (the text says with a jerk); fourth, arms spread sideways, bend the trunk to the left and right towards the calf; fifth, feet two feet apart, swing the trunk and outstretched arms in a semicircle to each side.
- Janu: standing, swing the foot forward with a knee jerk then back so the heel touches the buttock; ten times to begin with.
- Jangha: first, jumping up with the arms thrown up, coming down on the toes with feet apart (jumping jacks); second A, knees together, arms held forward, bend until thighs are parallel to the floor without lifting heels or toes; second B, heels raised, arms sideways, bend with knees apart without sitting on the heels.
- Pada-mula: A, rise and lower on the toes in a spring-like motion; B, balanced on the toes, jump up and land on the toes. This confirms the deck's "jump on toes".
- Gulpha-pada-pristha-pada-tala: hold one foot about nine inches off the ground and circle the ankle each way, then the other foot.
- Secondary corroboration for the knee and ankle forms: NIOS leg joint manual (different series, not the Board's) https://digital.nios.ac.in/content/373en/Pract/16.pdf
- The reprint also lists cure claims (rheumatism and so on), which were not used.

Asanas: no Board or MDNIY step text was reached. Gheranda Samhita translations place mandukasana at verse 2.34, uttana mandukasana at 2.35 and gomukhasana near 2.16 to 2.20 (Wikipedia and siva.sh summaries, secondary): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandukasana . Those descriptions differ from the deck's steps for mandukasana (deck has palms together and arms forward), which is why that frame is flagged.

Applied to deck: shoulder, trunk (fourth form corrected to side bends), knee, thigh (labels and steps) and ankle frames corrected to this text, with the Brahmachari reprint named on the shoulder frame.

Still open: Brahmachari trunk third form (fast jerk bends) has a safety question and no Board statement; steps and cautions for dandasana, sukhasana, mandukasana, uttana mandukasana, paschimottanasana, purvottanasana, gomukhasana, vipareetakarani, saral matsyasana and kati chakrasana from the MDNIY Yogasana monograph (Basavaraddi, 2016) or the Guidebook asana chapter.
