CORRECTED:
- Names and purpose (deck, not yet checked) | "  - **Names and purpose (checked: Guidebook ch.8 sec 8.1):** surya namaskara means salutation to the sun; it is a sequence of asanas, not a single asana. The Guidebook credits it with flexibility of the spine and limbs, circulation, correct breathing and less visceral fat, and says it prepares the body for asana. Digestion, immunity, detoxification and calm focus are not stated there; treat them as traditional well-being, not cure. The Guidebook says the sun-facing morning practice is custom, not mandatory."
- Other name forms in the deck (deck, not yet checked) | "  - **Other name forms in the deck (checked: Guidebook ch.8 sec 8.2):** the Guidebook names the arms-raised position hastottanasana (hasta uttanasana is the same), the inverted V parvatasana (adho mukha svanasana is the common name for the pose), and the eight-limb position sashtanga namaskara or ashtanga namaskara. The Guidebook sequence has no plank-like chaturanga dandasana; that is another school's sequence, so do not mix it with this one."
- Positions not repeated in the second half (deck, not yet checked) | "  - **Positions not repeated in the second half (checked: Guidebook ch.8 sec 8.2):** in the Guidebook sequence only two positions are not repeated, ashtanga namaskara and bhujangasana (postures six and seven). Chaturanga dandasana is not part of that sequence. Candidates who learned a plank-based sequence may give a third; follow the Board protocol sequence in the exam."
- Tips from the deck (deck, not yet checked) | "  - **Tips from the deck (checked: Guidebook ch.8 sec 8.2):** keep the movement slow, never jerky; keep the breath steady and rhythmic with no forceful breathing or breath holding; people with back problems practise only after advice from a yoga expert. The empty stomach tip is in the Guidebook's note on common misconceptions. Well-aired place is a general practice guideline in the Common Yoga Protocol."
