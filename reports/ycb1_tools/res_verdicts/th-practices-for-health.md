- Meaning of health (deck, not yet checked) | "  - **Meaning of health (deck, not yet checked) (checked: Guidebook ch.5 sec 5.1; WHO Constitution preamble):** the Guidebook quotes the World Health Organization as describing health as complete physical, mental, social and spiritual well-being, not only absence of disease. The WHO Constitution of 1948 itself names physical, mental and social well-being; 'spiritual' is added in the Guidebook and the deck, so state it as the Guidebook's wording or use the three-part form. In Sanskrit health is swasthya, from swa (self) and stha (abiding): abiding in one's own Self, described as sat-chit-ananda (the Guidebook)."
- Health according to yoga, in the deck (deck, not yet checked) | "  - **Health according to yoga, in the deck (deck, not yet checked) (checked: Guidebook ch.1 sec 1.6 and ch.5 sec 5.1):** relaxed muscles, loose joints that conserve energy, efficient use of energy, and organ systems that stay coordinated even under stress. A low metabolic rate is stated by the Guidebook as a feature of the body at rest, not as a general sign of health, so state it that way."
- Texts on health (deck, not yet checked) | "  - **Texts on health (deck, not yet checked) (checked: Guidebook ch.5 sec 5.1):** the Shvetashvatara Upanishad names lightness of body, health, freedom from craving, clear complexion, a beautiful voice, an agreeable odour and scanty excretions as first effects of entering yoga. The Guidebook adds that the Hatha Yoga Pradipika echoes these as marks of success. The Yoga Sutra, Vibhuti pada, speaks of beauty, grace, strength and adamantine hardness as perfection of the body, and of mastery of samana vayu leading to radiance (sutra 3.40 in standard editions; the Guidebook cites the pada)."
