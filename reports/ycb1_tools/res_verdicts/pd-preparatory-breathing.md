CONFIRMED:
- Abdominal breathing, other name | Guidebook ch.9 (Pranayama and Meditation): "abdominal or diaphragmatic breathing"; slow rhythmic and relaxing; slow deep abdominal breath breaks the stress cycle
- Combination or three-part breathing | Guidebook ch.9 Full Yogic Breath: one smooth flowing wave bottom to top on the inhale (abdomen, ribs/chest, collarbones) and top to bottom on the exhale
CORRECTED:
- Why practise first | "  - **Why practise first (checked: Guidebook ch.9, Board syllabus 5.1):** the Board syllabus lists abdominal, thoracic, clavicular breathing and the full yogic breath under pranayama, and the Guidebook calls the full yogic breath a basic need for restoring natural breathing; it says correct diaphragm movement brings better oxygen intake without effort. Calming the mind and lung capacity are general statements; joining with asanas is not stated in these sources."
- Chest breathing, other points | "  - **Chest breathing, other points (checked: Guidebook ch.9):** the ribcage widens in all directions on the inhale and returns on the exhale; keep the abdomen still and the shoulders relaxed. The Guidebook says it takes in less air than the abdominal breath and tends to be quick, and that it becomes dominant under stress, so slow abdominal breathing is the remedy. The three breaths are not just 'often combined': they unite in the full yogic breath. The word intercostal is not used in the Guidebook."
- Clavicular breathing, other points | "  - **Clavicular breathing, other points (checked: Guidebook ch.9):** the upper chest and collarbones lift on the inhale and lower on the exhale, with the shoulders kept from moving. The Guidebook calls this breath shallow and the last step before the full yogic breath; it describes it as typical of stress or breathing difficulty, so it is practised as a step towards the full breath and not on its own."
