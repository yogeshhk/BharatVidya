CORRECTED:
- Meaning of pranayama | "  - **Meaning of pranayama (checked: Guidebook ch.9):** the Guidebook splits it as prana (life force) plus ayama, glossed as control by stretching or expanding, and so reads it as control of the life force through the breath. It is the fourth of the eight limbs, after asana and before pratyahara (Guidebook ch.3 on Patanjali). The slide's Hindi note (extension, not reduction) and its English 'control of breath' are both ways of rendering the word."
- Named lists of pranayama | "  - **Named lists of pranayama (checked: Guidebook ch.9, Hatha Pradipika summary):** the Guidebook lists the eight Hatha Pradipika kumbhakas as surya bhedana, bhastrika, ujjayi, sheetali, sheetkari, bhramari, plavini and murcha, the same set as the notes give in a different order; it also names sahita and kevala as the two types of kumbhaka. Patanjali's definition (the breaking of the flow of inhalation and exhalation, tasmin sati svasa prasvasayoh gati vicchedah) is quoted in the Guidebook. The Gheranda Samhita list and Patanjali's four kinds still need a text."
- General benefits | "  - **General benefits (checked: Guidebook ch.9, Common Yoga Protocol):** the sources speak of calmness and peace of mind, a link between breath and mind, and lower stress from nadi shodhana. Lung capacity, nervous system and focus claims are general and not each stated; keep them as traditional or general benefits and not as medical claims."
- Kapalabhati (skull-shining breath) | "  - **Kapalabhati (skull-shining breath) (checked: Guidebook ch.7 and ch.9):** the Guidebook classes it as a cleansing kriya of the Hatha texts (shatkriyas), notes that many treat it as a pranayama because it involves breathing, and says its exhalation is forceful and rapid, reversing the usual pattern of active inhale and passive exhale. It is not in the Board syllabus list of pranayamas. The one to two minutes a day and the blood pressure caution are not stated in the sources read; the Guidebook does advise people with heart disease or high blood pressure to avoid forceful bhastrika."
- Bhramari timing | "  - **Bhramari timing (checked: Common Yoga Protocol):** the protocol gives 5 rounds, not minutes; it describes bhramari as relieving stress and anxiety and as a preparation for concentration and meditation. Use the protocol count."
- Sheetali timing and use | "  - **Sheetali timing and use (checked: Guidebook ch.9):** sheetali means cooling; it cools body and mind and calms the mind. Hot weather is consistent with this but the Guidebook does not state it; the Guidebook notes the variation sheetkari (inhale through the teeth) for those who cannot roll the tongue, and the cautions for cold, cough, asthma, tonsillitis and low blood pressure."
- Heating and cooling | "  - **Heating and cooling (checked: Guidebook ch.9):** sheetali and sheetkari are cooling; chandrabhedana is the cooling left-nostril breath and suryabhedana is the warming one. Bhastrika as 'heating' is not stated in the passages read, so that quiz answer needs a text."
