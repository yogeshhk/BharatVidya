- Vedantic key terms (deck, not yet checked) | "  - **Vedantic key terms (deck, not yet checked) (checked: Guidebook ch.1 sec 1.4, ch.3 sec 3.3, ch.5 sec 5.1):** the Self is described in the Guidebook as sat-chit-ananda, being, consciousness and bliss. In Samkhya-Yoga, purusha is pure consciousness and prakriti is matter, made of the three gunas. Ishvara in Patanjali's Sutra is a special purusha, not a religious figure, and the Upanishads deal with jiva, jagat and Ishvara. Avidya is ignorance of one's true nature (the first of the five kleshas). The terms Brahman, maya, upadhi as set out on the slide are standard Advaita glossary terms not defined in these sources."
- Three bodies in short (deck, not yet checked) | "  - **Three bodies in short (deck, not yet checked) (checked: Guidebook ch.5 sec 5.2):** the Guidebook ties the three bodies to the sheaths: the physical (gross) body is the annamaya sheath; the subtle body is made of the pranamaya, manomaya and vijnanamaya sheaths; the causal body is the anandamaya sheath. So the subtle body is more than mind, intellect and ego; it includes the vital sheath. The slide's 'seed of individuality and karma' for the causal body is the slide's wording, not the Guidebook's."
- Five sheaths in short (deck, not yet checked) | "  - **Five sheaths in short (deck, not yet checked) (checked: Guidebook ch.1 sec 1.6 Pancha Kosha and ch.5 sec 5.2):** annamaya is the outermost, food-made physical sheath; pranamaya is the energy sheath, the bridge between body and mind; manomaya is mind, thinking and emotion (manas, ahamkara and lower buddhi); vijnanamaya is higher intellect and intuition; anandamaya is the bliss sheath, closest to the Real Self but still a sheath (the Self lies behind all five). The Taittiriya Upanishad speaks of five brahma manifestations and Adi Shankaracharya turned them into the five koshas, so it is the textual source of the model; the Guidebook uses the model in its health chapter."

The usual list is speech, hands, feet, excretion and reproduction. The slide's pairing of each organ with one of the five elements is not standard in all sources; present the list without the pairing."
- Pancha prana, the five vital airs (deck, not yet checked) | "  - **Pancha prana, the five vital airs (deck, not yet checked) (checked: Guidebook ch.5 sec 5.2; web search on prana locations):** the five major pranas are prana, apana, udana, samana and vyana and the five upapranas are naga, kurma, krikala, devadatta and dhananjaya. Locations vary among sources: prana is commonly placed in the chest or heart region, apana in the lower abdomen or pelvis, samana near the navel, udana in the throat and head, vyana throughout the body. The slide's prana from head to navel and udana from navel to head do not match the usual accounts. Describe dhananjaya by its common functions (for example heart and tissue support), not as 'remaining after death', unless a source is cited. The Guidebook lists the names only."
