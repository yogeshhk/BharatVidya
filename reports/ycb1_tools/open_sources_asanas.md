## Open sources for eight Level 1 asanas

Research date: 2026-11-24. All text below is a short paraphrase in my own words. Health claims quoted from a source are marked as the source's claim and are not to be repeated in the booklet.

## How the sources were used

- Satyananda Saraswati, Asana Pranayama Mudra Bandha (Bihar School of Yoga, 2006), full text at https://jainqq.org/booktext/Asan_Pranayam_Mudra_Bandh/009366 . This is a Board-named book. Page numbers below are the printed book pages; the site's own "Page #" marker runs about 8 to 9 higher, so a reader can find the same spot either way. Treat the numbers as approximate (plus or minus 1).
- CBSE Class XI Yoga handbook, https://www.cbse.gov.in/cbsenew/skill-education/books/YogaXI.pdf (secondary, government). Its syllabus list contains all eight names in the Board's spelling (Mandukasana, Uttana Mandukasana, Purvottanasana, Dandasana, Kati Chakrasana, Gomukhasana, Vipareetkaraniasana, Saralmatsyasana), but it gives step-by-step text only for Gomukhasana and Mandukasana.
- NIOS Yoga Level A, B and C (OBE Bharatiya Jnana Parampara), chapters on asanas, for example https://www.nios.ac.in/media/documents/OBE_indian_knowledge_tradition/Level_B/Yoga/Yoga_(Level-B)_ch-5-final.pdf (secondary, government).
- Not reachable or not usable: Iyengar Light on Yoga and Tiwari Asana Why and How (copyright, no free text found), Dhirendra Brahmachari (no online text found), MDNIY monograph (priced), Kaivalyadhama web pages (no page for these asanas surfaced in search).

## 1. Mandukasana

- Names: Mandukasana, frog pose (CBSE). Satyananda has no asana of this name; its only match is Manduki Mudra (frog gesture, a different practice, printed page about 459). Not the Satyananda frog pose.
- Steps (CBSE handbook, section on Mandukasana): sit in Vajrasana. Make fists with both hands, thumbs tucked inside the fingers, and press the fists against the navel. Breathe out and bend forward, keeping the gaze ahead. Hold the breath out in the bent position, then breathe in and come back up. Repeat three to four times.
- Breathing and hold: breathe out while bending, hold the breath out, breathe in to return. Hold "for some time", no number given.
- Benefits as the source states them: flexibility of thighs and legs, less fat on thighs and hips, better digestion and excretion. The source also claims it raises insulin, helps diabetes, helps heart disease and cures ankle, knee and back pain. Those are the source's claims; do not repeat them.
- Cautions: abdominal injury, any back pain, knee injury (CBSE).
- Source and tag: CBSE Class XI Yoga handbook, Mandukasana section (secondary). Not found in a Board-named book; there is no step text in Satyananda.
- Doubt: sources on the web also use "frog pose" for a different, prone hip-opening pose. The CBSE version is the Vajrasana fist version and matches the Board syllabus list, which places it next to Vajrasana and Ushtrasana.

## 2. Uttana Mandukasana

- Names: Uttana Mandukasana, upright frog (CBSE syllabus list and a government Vikaspedia page, which says the head is supported by the elbows).
- Steps (secondary, PharmEasy health blog, https://pharmeasy.in/blog/health-fitness-benefits-of-uttana-mandukasana-and-how-to-do-it/): sit in Vajrasana and spread the knees apart. Fold one arm, bring the hand over the opposite shoulder and rest the palm on the shoulder blade. Switch arms. Hold, then release slowly. This agrees with the usual Indian description (arms folded behind the head, elbows as support).
- Breathing and hold: not given by that source. Common Yoga Protocol advice is not to hold the breath unless stated.
- Benefits: the blog lists neck, stress, flexibility and others, and itself says most lack strong evidence. Do not repeat them.
- Cautions: hip joint problem, pregnancy, menstruation; hernia, arthritis or back problems only under a trained teacher (same blog).
- Source and tag: secondary only (commercial health blog plus the CBSE name list). Weak. Not found in a Board-named book or in NIOS. Needs a better source, for example the MDNIY monograph or Tiwari.

## 3. Purvottanasana

- Names: Purvottanasana, Poorvottanasana, upward plank, inclined plane (Art of Living page and other sites). Satyananda has no pose of this name.
- Steps (Art of Living, https://www.artofliving.org/us-en/yoga/yoga-poses/upward-plank-poorvottanasana): sit with legs out, feet together, back upright. Put the palms on the floor beside the hips with arms straight. Lean back, let the hands take the weight, then lift the hips so the body is one straight line, feet on the floor, head tilted back. Breathe steadily while holding. Lower the hips and sit. Repeat with fingers pointing the other way.
- Breathing and hold: breathe in while lifting, out while lowering, steady breath in the hold. That page gives no hold time. Other sites say about five breaths.
- Disagreement: Art of Living says the fingers face forward in the setup and then asks for the opposite direction on the repeat. Other sites say the fingers point back (towards the feet) in the main version. State fingers direction only after a Board-named book is checked.
- Benefits as stated: strengthens wrists, arms, shoulders, back and spine; stretches legs and hips. The page also claims thyroid stimulation and help with breathing. Do not repeat those.
- Cautions: wrist injury, neck injury (use support or skip). Other sites add carpal tunnel and neck problems.
- Source and tag: secondary (Art of Living, a large yoga organisation). Not found in a Board-named book.

## 4. Gomukhasana

- Names: Gomukhasana, cow face pose (Satyananda, NIOS, CBSE).
- Satyananda (Board-named), printed pages about 225 to 226: sit in the folded-leg seat with the right knee directly above the left. Put the left arm behind the back and the right arm over the right shoulder, and try to clasp the fingers behind the back. Bring the raised elbow behind the head so the head presses against the inside of the arm. Keep the spine upright. Stay up to 2 minutes, then undo and repeat on the other side. Breathing normal.
- NIOS Level C (secondary), Gomukhasana section: bend the left leg under so the heel touches the right buttock, put the right leg over it so the right heel touches the left buttock, right knee above left. Same arm and head instructions. Hold one minute with normal breathing, then repeat on the other side.
- CBSE handbook (secondary): the same leg crossing, fingers clasped behind the back, head upright against the raised arm, look up and breathe normally, repeat on the other side. Its steps do not give a time.
- Where they differ: hold time (Satyananda up to 2 minutes, NIOS 1 minute); Satyananda starts from a folded seat, NIOS and CBSE from Dandasana. Use Satyananda as the Board-named one.
- Benefits as stated: Satyananda says it helps relaxation, eases leg cramp, loosens shoulders and neck, opens the chest and improves posture; it also claims relief of backache, sciatica, rheumatism and diabetes and kidney effects (source claims, do not repeat). CBSE: mobility of shoulders and hips, strength of chest and back muscles, deeper breathing.
- Cautions: Satyananda gives none for this pose. CBSE section text lists severe back or neck injury, blood pressure problems and migraine near this pose, but the heading on that block is ambiguous, so treat it as a general caution only. A government MEA yoga page says people with bleeding piles should avoid it.
- Tag: Board-named book (Satyananda) plus secondary (NIOS Level C, CBSE).

## 5. Dandasana

- Names: Dandasana, staff pose; NIOS calls it the starting position (sthiti) for sitting asanas, with Shithila Dandasana as the relaxed version. Satyananda has no plain Dandasana (only Merudandasana, a different pose, printed page about 303). Not that pose.
- Steps (NIOS Level A, Level B and Level C, asana chapters): sit with both legs stretched out and heels together. Put the palms on the floor beside the buttocks. Keep spine, neck and head upright. Close the eyes.
- Shithila Dandasana (NIOS): sit with legs a little apart, lean the trunk slightly back, hands on the floor behind for support, relax.
- Breathing and hold: not stated. It is a starting posture.
- Benefits and cautions: not stated in NIOS for this pose.
- Source and tag: NIOS (secondary, government). Not found in a Board-named book.

## 6. Viparita Karani

- Names: Satyananda calls it Vipareeta Karani Asana (inverted pose). The CBSE list spells it Vipareetkaraniasana, and NIOS Level B and C spell it Viparitakarani and list it as a basic supine asana. Satyananda also has Vipareeta Karani Mudra, a separate practice built on this pose (printed pages about 453 to 455).
- Steps (Satyananda, printed pages 264 to 265): lie flat, legs together, palms down by the sides. Raise the straight legs over the body, push on the arms to lift the hips, roll the spine up, turn the palms up and bend the elbows. Rest the hips on the hands near the wrists so the hands cup the hips. Bring the legs to vertical and relax the feet. The trunk ends at about 45 degrees, weight on shoulders, neck and elbows, chin not pressed into the chest. To come down, lower the legs over the head, hands down, lower the spine bone by bone, do not lift the head, lower straight legs. Rest in shavasana.
- Breathing and hold: breathe in lying, hold the breath in while going up, then normal or ujjayi breathing in the pose, hold the breath in while coming down. Beginners a few seconds, building over weeks up to 3 to 5 minutes. Practise once per session.
- Practice note from the source: it prepares for sarvangasana and suits beginners or stiff necks; pad the elbows if sore; knees may bend at first.
- Benefits: the source points to the sarvangasana list (thyroid, circulation, calm mind, and many therapy claims). Those are the source's claims; do not repeat them.
- Cautions: Satyananda says "as for sarvangasana": enlarged thyroid, liver or spleen; neck disc or spondylitis; high blood pressure or heart problems; weak eye blood vessels; thrombosis; avoid in menstruation and late pregnancy.
- NIOS Level B and C (secondary) show it in a figure with Sarvangasana but give no steps in the text found.
- Tag: Board-named book (Satyananda). Note that the version above is the supported 45 degree shoulder-and-hips pose, which differs from the legs-up-the-wall form; flag the booklet version.

## 7. Saral Matsyasana

- Names: CBSE syllabus spells it Saralmatsyasana. Not found under that name in any open source. Satyananda has Matsyasana (fish pose) with easier variations, and the Satyananda Variation 3 (for beginners) is the closest match.
- Satyananda (Board-named), printed pages 184 to 186, variation 3: stretch both legs straight, lean back on the arms, rest the top of the head on the floor, arch the back, hands on thighs or floor, return after a while. Variation 2 (beginners) keeps one leg straight and one foot on the opposite thigh, holding that foot.
- Satyananda full Matsyasana, for comparison: from the lotus seat, lean back on arms and elbows, lower the crown of the head, hold the big toes, breathe slowly and deeply. Hold up to 5 minutes, 1 to 3 is enough. Counter pose: halasana or sarvangasana. Move into and out of it slowly with the arms.
- NIOS Level B (secondary): the full pose is from the lotus seat, held one minute with normal breathing, return, rest in shavasana.
- Web sources (secondary, for example Healthline and Yogapoint, not checked in detail): the straight-leg version lies on the back, hands under hips, elbows pressed down, chest lifted, crown or back of head touching, a few seconds to about 15 seconds at first.
- Benefits as stated: Satyananda claims help for many conditions (abdominal, asthma, thyroid, immunity). Do not repeat. NIOS only says it complements shoulder stand and helps breathing.
- Cautions (Satyananda): heart disease, peptic ulcer, hernia, back conditions, any serious illness, pregnancy. Web sources add high or low blood pressure and a delicate neck.
- Doubt: "saral" is not verified as a Board term. The "easy fish" reading is an inference from the name. Treat as partially found.
- Tag: Board-named book (full pose and beginner variations) plus secondary.

## 8. Kati Chakrasana

- Names: Satyananda has Kati Chakrasana (waist rotating pose), a standing twist. NIOS Level A, B and C have Ardha Kati Chakrasana (half waist wheel pose), a standing side bend. These are two different poses. The CBSE list names plain Kati Chakrasana, so the twist is the likely match.
- Steps (Satyananda, printed pages 142 to 143): stand with feet about half a metre apart. Breathe in while raising the arms to shoulder level. Breathe out and twist to the left. Bring the right hand to the left shoulder and wrap the left arm around the back to the right side of the waist. Look over the left shoulder, neck straight, feet fixed. Hold the breath out for about 2 seconds and deepen the twist. Breathe in and return. Repeat on the other side. That is one round. Keep it relaxed, no jerking.
- Breathing and duration: breathe in raising the arms, out twisting, hold the breath out about 2 seconds, in on return. Five to ten rounds.
- Benefits as stated: tones waist, back and hips; helps stiffness and posture; relaxing, may ease tension. No cure claims beyond that.
- Cautions: none stated for this pose in Satyananda.
- Ardha Kati Chakrasana (NIOS Level A, about page 58 to 59): from Tadasana, raise the right arm and breathe in until the arm touches the ear, then breathe out and bend the trunk to the left, sliding the left hand down the thigh; keep elbows and knees straight and bend only sideways; hold about a minute with normal breathing; return and repeat on the other side. NIOS benefits: spine flexibility and sideways bending, less fat around the waist, liver function (source claim, do not repeat).
- Tag: Board-named book (Satyananda, twist) and secondary (NIOS, side bend). Flag to the author which one the booklet means.

## Gaps

- Mandukasana, Uttana Mandukasana, Purvottanasana, Dandasana: no text in Satyananda under these names. Tiwari, Iyengar and Dhirendra Brahmachari are not freely readable here; they are the next place to look.
- Uttana Mandukasana is the weakest (blog only).
