# Pass 4: Patanjala Yoga Sutra, deck bullets only (ycb1-tx-patanjala-yoga-sutra)

Sources:
- YS: Yoga Sutra, GRETIL plain text "sa_pataJjali-yogasUtra" (Agashe 1904 edition, sutras extracted by P. A. Maas).
- Guidebook: the official guidebook, ch.3 sec 3.2 (chitta and chitta bhumis) and its prayer page (local copy).
- Web: the Wikipedia articles "Yoga Sutras of Patanjali" and "Patanjali", which summarise Bryant and Maas.

## Items

- **Patanjali (deck)**: CORRECTED.
  - Old: "his dates are uncertain (scholars place the text around the start of the common era)"
  - New: "his dates are uncertain: scholars place the text anywhere from the second century BCE to about 400 CE, and many put it near the start of the common era"
  - Source: Wikipedia "Yoga Sutras of Patanjali", which cites Bryant (most scholars, first to second century CE) and Maas (about 400 CE).
  - The rest of the bullet is confirmed. The tradition crediting him with grammar and medicine is the guidebook's prayer ("Yoga for healthy mind and body, medicine for physical ailments, grammar for the speech"), with Wikipedia "Patanjali" as the second source. Keep "traditionally".
- **Size of the four padas (deck)**: CONFIRMED.
  - GRETIL (Agashe 1904) has 51, 55, 55 and 34 sutras, 195 in all. Keep "counts vary slightly between editions".
- **The mind (chitta) (deck)**: CORRECTED.
  - Old: "its working has four parts: chitta (memory and impressions), buddhi (decision), ahamkara (ego), manas (the synthesising, sensing mind)."
  - New: "the Upanishads speak of four parts of the inner instrument (antahkarana): chitta (memory and impressions), buddhi (decision), ahamkara (ego), manas (the synthesising mind); Patanjali uses chitta for the whole mind."
  - Source: Guidebook ch.3 sec 3.2.
- **Five states of the mind (chitta bhumi) (deck)**: CORRECTED.
  - Old: "vikshipta (occasionally steady, rajasic) ... niruddha (stilled, sattvic)"
  - New: "vikshipta (occasionally steady: mostly sattvic, but rajas pulls it away) ... niruddha (stilled: all movements, even the sattvic ones, are held)"
  - Source: Guidebook ch.3 sec 3.2, which quotes the Vyasa commentary on 1.1: vikshipta is "sattvadhikyena ... rajomatraya", and niruddha is the state where all movements are restrained.
  - Confirmed in the same bullet: kshipta (rajas), mudha (tamas), ekagra, and that ekagra and niruddha count as yoga.
- **Five movements of the mind (deck)**: CONFIRMED.
  - YS 1.6 (pramana, viparyaya, vikalpa, nidra, smriti) and 1.7 (pratyaksha, anumana, agama).
- **Other contents of the first pada (deck)**: CONFIRMED.
  - YS 1.12 (abhyasa and vairagya), 1.30 and 1.31 (nine obstacles, four companions), 1.33 (chitta prasadana), 1.17 and 1.18 (samprajnata, and the other).
- **Aim (deck)**: CORRECTED.
  - Old: "the eight-limbed path is the way, called the royal path (raja yoga), which takes in the ideas of the other yogas."
  - New: "the eight-limbed path is the way (2.29); later tradition calls this path raja yoga."
  - Source: YS 2.29 and 4.34 (kaivalya); Guidebook ch.3 ("The aim ... is Kaivalya"). "Takes in the ideas of the other yogas" has no source, so drop it.
