## Items

- **Apathya food in the text (CORE line)**: CORRECTED.
  - Old: "food that is too bitter, sour, pungent, salty or hot, stale or reheated, dry, or heavy and hard to digest; alcohol and, in the text, meat and fish; also overeating."
  - New: "food that is bitter, sour, pungent, salty or hot; reheated, dry or over-salted; fermented or oily; alcohol, fish, meat, curds, garlic and onion; also overeating."
  - Source: HP chapter one, "Foods injurious to a Yogi". The text says nothing of "heavy and hard to digest", so drop it.
- **Foods to avoid, the word "fermented"** (pass 3 left it "only partly supported"): CORRECTED.
  - Old: "the slide's 'fermented' is only partly supported (sour gruel, curds, buttermilk)."
  - New: "fermented food is named in the list."
  - Source: HP chapter one, "Foods injurious to a Yogi": "Bitter, sour, saltish, green vegetables, fermented, oily ...".
- **Foods that suit**: CONFIRMED.
  - HP chapter one: wheat, rice, barley, milk, ghee, sugar candy, honey, dried ginger, the five vegetables, moong (green gram) and pure water.
- **Six tastes, named (deck)**: CONFIRMED for the names, with two edits.
  - The six are madhura (sweet), amla (sour), lavana (salty), katu (pungent), tikta (bitter) and kashaya (astringent). Source: Charaka Sutrasthana 26 (Atreyabhadrakapyiya Adhyaya), which settles the number of tastes at six, and the carakasamhitaonline page "Rasa".
  - The lean to sweet is supported: HP chapter one says the food of mitahara is "well cooked with ghee and sweets".
  - Edit 1: the dosha sentence stays out of the exam text, as the notes already say.
  - Edit 2: drop "(to be checked before use)" for the names.
- **Sitting after meals (deck)**: CORRECTED.
  - Old: "vajrasana is the posture commonly allowed soon after food; other postures are done on an empty stomach."
  - New: "practise on an empty or light stomach, and eat only 20 to 30 minutes after practice."
  - Source: CYP, "Before the practice" and "After practice". No Board, Ministry of Ayush or primary-text source was found for vajrasana after meals; it appears only on wellness and insurance sites. A Government of NCT of Delhi AYUSH booklet ("Yoga for Family") advises waiting two to three hours after a large meal.
- **One fourth or one third empty (open variation)**: CONFIRMED as a genuine variation.
  - The hatha texts give one fourth. HP chapter one: "3/4 of hunger is satisfied". GS fifth lesson, verse 22: half the stomach food, one quarter water, one quarter empty.
  - Ayurveda divides the stomach into three parts and keeps one empty. Source: Charaka Vimanasthana 2, Trividhakukshiya Vimana.
  - The Board syllabus names neither scheme. Teach one fourth for the hatha texts, and mention one third as the Ayurveda answer.
