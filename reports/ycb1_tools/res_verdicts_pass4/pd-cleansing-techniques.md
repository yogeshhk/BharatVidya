## Open items

- **Antar and bahir trataka (inside "Other cleansing actions known by name", deck, not yet checked)**. REMOVE-OR-MARK-UNVERIFIED. Searched: Hatha Pradipika 2.31 to 2.32 (gaze steadily at a small mark till the eyes fill with tears; trataka removes eye troubles and sloth) and Gheranda Samhita 1.53 to 1.54 (gaze without winking at a small object till tears flow). Neither text splits trataka into antar and bahir. The split appears only on yoga-school pages. Remove the split. Trataka is also outside the Board list for this unit (4.2: knowledge of dhauti and neti, practice of kapalabhati), so keep it as a one-line name only.
- **Trataka one-liner ("steady gazing at a small mark without blinking, traditionally till the eyes water")**. CONFIRMED. Hatha Pradipika 2.31 (Sinh 1915); Gheranda Samhita 1.53 (Vasu 1915).
- **The six cleansing actions named** (nauli, trataka, basti alongside dhauti, neti, kapalabhati). CONFIRMED. Hatha Pradipika 2.22 lists dhauti, basti, neti, trataka, nauli and kapalabhati (Sinh 1915).
- **Kapalabhati technique and count** (pass 3 confirmed). CONFIRMED. Common Yoga Protocol: 30 rapid breaths per round, 3 rounds; beginners up to 3 rounds of 20 breaths; active exhalation, passive inhalation.
- **Kapalabhati cautions** (cardiac conditions, giddiness, high blood pressure, vertigo, chronic nose bleeding, epilepsy, migraine, stroke, hernia, gastric ulcers). CONFIRMED. Common Yoga Protocol, Kapalabhati, "A word of caution".
- **Kapalabhati in the Hatha Pradipika**. CONFIRMED for the form: Hatha Pradipika 2.35, quick inhalation and exhalation like a blacksmith's bellows. The text credits it with drying up phlegm disorders: a traditional claim, not to be repeated as a health claim.
- **"Dhauti and neti: to be written" line**. Note only: it is stale; the dhauti and neti bullets below it now carry the content. Delete the line when the block is edited.
