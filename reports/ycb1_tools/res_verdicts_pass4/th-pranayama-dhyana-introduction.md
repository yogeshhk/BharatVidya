# Pass 4 verdicts: ycb1-th-pranayama-dhyana-introduction

- Dhyana aims in the deck: emotional well-being and better attention | CONFIRMED (in well-being wording) | Common Yoga Protocol (Ministry of Ayush), "Dhyana in Shambhavi Mudra", Benefits: helps the practitioner let go of negative emotions and develop positive ones, keeps the mind calm and quiet, increases concentration, memory, clarity of thought and will power. Teach it as well-being, not as treatment of a disorder.
- Check before starting (meditation) | CORRECTED | Old: "eyes closed; trunk straight; head, neck and back in line; shoulders and belly relaxed; hands resting on the knees in a mudra; body still; mood calm." New: "sit in a meditative posture with the spine comfortably erect; hands in jnana mudra, palms up on the thighs; arms and shoulders loose and relaxed; eyes closed, face slightly upturned; a calm and quiet place, with a relaxed body and mind." Source: Common Yoga Protocol, "Dhyana in Shambhavi Mudra" (technique) and "General Guidelines for Yoga Practice" (before the practice).
- Two ways of practice: concentration versus mindfulness | REMOVE-OR-MARK-UNVERIFIED | Searched the Board syllabus, the Common Yoga Protocol and the Guidebook: the split is not stated (the Protocol says "you need not concentrate; just maintain a mild focus between the eyebrows and be conscious of your breath"). It comes from research literature, not the Board's sources; keep it out of the exam content.
- For beginners | CORRECTED | Old: "a fixed time and a quiet place, a few minutes at first and then more, a comfortable seat with the back erect; watch the breath; if sleepy, take a deep breath and breathe out firmly." New: "a calm and quiet place, the spine erect, awareness of the breath; soothing music may be played in the background; stay as long as you can, and practise regularly, since results take time." Source: Common Yoga Protocol, "Dhyana in Shambhavi Mudra" (note for beginners) and "General Guidelines for Yoga Practice" (persistent and regular practice). The sleepiness tip was not found in a named source; drop it.
- Health benefits of meditation (as traditionally taught) | CORRECTED | Old: "calm, clarity and better concentration; less reaction to stress." New: "calm, clarity, better concentration, memory and will power; helps let go of negative emotions such as fear and anger." Source: Common Yoga Protocol, Dhyana benefits. "Less reaction to stress" as a measured effect is mixed in the evidence (NCCIH, "Meditation and Mindfulness: Effectiveness and Safety"), so it is not stated.
