# Pass 4: Hatha Pradipika (ycb1-tx-hatha-pradipika)

Sources read in full for this pass:
- HP: Hatha Yoga Pradipika, Pancham Sinh translation (1914), Wikisource pages "Hatha Yoga Pradipika/1" to "/4" (raw text). Wikisource numbers no verses, so chapter and section headings are given, not verse numbers.
- GS: The Gheranda Samhita, Sris Chandra Vasu translation, in Sacred Books of the Hindus vol. XV (1915), archive.org item india.history.resource.88861 (OCR text).

## Items

- **Asanas in the text (CORE line)**: CORRECTED.
  - Old: "Others described include svastikasana, gomukhasana, virasana, kurmasana, kukkutasana, dhanurasana, matsyasana, paschimottanasana, mayurasana and shavasana."
  - New: "The other eleven are svastikasana, gomukhasana, virasana, kurmasana, kukkutasana, uttana kurmasana, dhanurasana, matsyendrasana, paschimottanasana, mayurasana and shavasana."
  - Source: HP chapter one, asana headings. The translation heads one of them "Matsya-asana", but the posture is the twist "as explained by Sri Matsyanatha", so it is matsyendrasana, not the modern fish pose.
  - Already right in the same line: "Siva taught 84 asanas" and the first four (siddha, padma, simha, bhadra). Siddhasana: "being very comfortable, one should always practice it" (HP chapter one).
- **Chapter on samadhi: further content (deck)**: CONFIRMED.
  - HP chapter four: "In all the Yogas, there are four states: arambha, ghata, parichaya, nispatti", each with its own heading.
  - The same chapter covers laya (absorption) and nada (the inner sound) and its stages.
  - Drop "(to be checked)".
- **Gheranda, Shatkarma (deck)**: CONFIRMED.
  - GS first lesson: the six are dhauti, basti, neti, lauliki (nauli), trataka, kapalabhati.
- **Gheranda, Asana (deck)**: CORRECTED.
  - Old: "thirty-two postures, grouped by use as meditative, relaxing and culture-building (body-strengthening); steadiness and comfort are stressed."
  - New: "thirty-two postures, chosen out of eighty-four as useful for people (the grouping into meditative, relaxing and culture-building is a modern teaching aid, not the text's)."
  - Source: GS second lesson: "Among them eighty-four are the best; and among these eighty-four, thirty-two have been found useful for mankind."
- **Gheranda, Mudra (deck)**: CONFIRMED.
  - GS third lesson opens with twenty-five mudras. Maha mudra, nabho mudra, khechari, viparita karani and yoni mudra are all in the list.
- **Gheranda, Pratyahara (deck)**: CORRECTED.
  - Old: "withdrawal of the senses, as preparation for meditation (the exact techniques named in the deck are to be checked)."
  - New: "bring the wandering mind back from each kind of sense object (sights, praise and blame, smells, tastes and the rest) under the control of the Self."
  - Source: GS fourth lesson, a short chapter. The deck's list (yoni mudra, shambhavi, the five dharanas) belongs to the chapter on mudras, so drop it.
- **Gheranda, Pranayama (deck)**: CORRECTED.
  - Old: "advice on diet first, then eight kumbhakas (sahita, surya bheda, ujjayi, sitali, bhastrika, bhramari, murchha, kevali), with signs of success."
  - New: "four needs first (a good place, a suitable time, moderate food, purified nadis), then eight kumbhakas (sahita, surya bheda, ujjayi, sitali, bhastrika, bhramari, murchha, kevali), with signs of success."
  - Source: GS fifth lesson, verse 2 (the four things) and verse 46 (the eight kinds). The names match exactly.
- **Gheranda, Dhyana (deck)**: CONFIRMED.
  - GS sixth lesson, verse 1: three kinds. Sthula is a form such as one's guru or deity, jyotis is light, and sukshma is a point (bindu) and the kundali.
  - Spelling variants: jyotis or jyotir. The 1915 text uses both.
- **Gheranda, Samadhi (deck)**: CONFIRMED.
  - Pass 3 already confirmed six kinds from the seventh lesson. Nothing new was found.
