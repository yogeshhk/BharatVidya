# Pass 4 verdicts: ycb1-pt-teaching-groups (Principles of teaching to different groups)

Sources used in this file:
- NCPCR Guidelines for Child Yoga, as reproduced on Vikaspedia, the Government of India portal (https://en.vikaspedia.in/viewcontent/health/ayush/yoga-1/guidelines-for-child-yoga): sections for children aged 3 to 10 and for adolescents up to 18.
- Board Syllabus for Geriatric Yoga aka Chair Yoga (Chair as Prop), docs/Syllabus-for-Chair-Yoga.pdf.
- Common Yoga Protocol, Ministry of Ayush (2019 edition at mea.gov.in; 2015 copy in docs), General Guidelines.
- Board Level 1 syllabus (docs), Teaching Skills unit: the six groups.

## Open items

- **Youth (CORE, not confirmed).** CORRECTED.
  - Old: "more energy and range; practices that build strength, focus and balance."
  - New: "no competition; move from easy to harder practices without haste or force; movement between postures builds strength, endurance and flexibility; explain each practice and its benefit; encourage, since body image may be a worry."
  - Source: NCPCR Guidelines for Child Yoga, adolescents section. Note: the guideline covers adolescents up to 18; the syllabus word "youth" may be wider, so keep it to these points.
  - This also settles the deck versus notes conflict on "endurance": the source names strength, endurance and flexibility.
- **Older adults (geriatric) (CORE, not confirmed).** CORRECTED.
  - Old: "slower, supported, seated or against a wall; gentle movement; shorter holds."
  - New: "slower and gentle; supported practice, often seated with a chair as a prop; within the person's capacity; a physician or yoga therapist first in chronic disease, pain or heart trouble."
  - Source: the Board's own Syllabus for Geriatric Yoga aka Chair Yoga (the chair as a prop, safety and risk reduction); Common Yoga Protocol General Guidelines (own capacity; consult a physician or yoga therapist in chronic disease, pain or cardiac problems). "Against a wall" and "shorter holds" were not found in a named source: dropped. The MDNIY booklet "Yoga for Geriatric Population" exists (yogamdniy.nic.in) but could not be opened here; read it in a later pass.
- **Children (hand-written).** CORRECTED to add the source facts.
  - Old: "short, playful and lively practices, matched to their age; keep the attention span in mind."
  - New: "short, playful and lively practices, matched to their age; teach through imaginative stories; a session of not more than 20 minutes; no long holds and no breath holding (kumbhaka)."
  - Source: NCPCR Guidelines for Child Yoga (children aged 3 to 10: session not more than 20 minutes; imaginative stories are the best method; no long holds or kumbhaka; under 10 years, up to about 10 seconds in the final posture).
- **Women (hand-written).** CORRECTED.
  - Old: "take note of cycle, pregnancy and the body's needs; avoid the practices that are cautioned."
  - New: "during pregnancy and menstruation, consult a yoga expert before practice; avoid the practices cautioned for these states."
  - Source: Common Yoga Protocol General Guidelines; the asana pages of the protocol list the cautions for pregnancy and menstruation.
- **Special attention group (hand-written).** CONFIRMED in substance. Common Yoga Protocol General Guidelines (physician or yoga therapist first in chronic disease, pain, cardiac problems); the group teaching point was checked in pass 3 against the guidebook.
- **Adapting by group, in short (deck, not yet checked).** CONFIRMED for the group names (Board syllabus) and for children, youth and older adults by the sources above; the youth and older-adult wording follows the CORRECTED lines.
- **FAQ "start in silence" versus prayer.** CONFIRMED as both right: Common Yoga Protocol says start with a prayer; it ends in meditation or deep silence. Teach: settle in silence, then the prayer.
